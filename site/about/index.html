Why use a workskin?

Use formatting
readers recognize.

Make the medium recognizable. Give messages chat bubbles, letters a paper background, and case files report headings.

Help readers follow the scene. Distinct speakers, timestamps, stanzas, and section labels give complicated passages a clear rhythm.

Keep labels readable. These examples use real text and simple HTML. Try hiding the skins below: names, dates, and reading order stay with the content.

Write first, reuse the format

Type the conversation.

Type NAME: text and a Svelte component turns it into a chat log. Add a time, quoted reply, or reaction when the scene needs it. Try the other formats in the menu, too.

In EO3, your writing, the reusable component, and the workskin are separate modules. Use the same component for another conversation or document, with its own text input.

How to use an example

  1. 01

    Open & write

    Choose an example and edit “Write here.” The component builds the HTML. Change colors and spacing in the Workskin module.

  2. 02

    Check the preview

    Watch the AO3 preview as you edit. EO3 applies a tested copy of AO3’s HTML and CSS processing rules. Type the title, author, and chapter title straight into the preview; it counts your words and chapters as you go.

  3. 03

    Take it to AO3

    Use “Copy Workskin CSS” to create a Work Skin on AO3, choose it for your work, then use “Copy HTML” in the chapter’s HTML editor.

Chapters and reusable modules

Keep chapters and
styles together.

EO3 opens in a simple view: each chapter is a list of your text, styles, and ready-made blocks. Keep chapters together, share a stylesheet across the work, or style one chapter separately. Switch to the nodes view to connect the modules yourself.

Add any of these examples to an existing fic. In the simple view, choose “+ add” → “Blocks.” In the nodes view, search for it in “add node,” or open “Groups and templates” there, then connect its Compose and Workskin modules to your chapter. Its writing input and renderer are already linked.

AO3 is the focus of this collection. EO3 also has targets for wafrn, custom sites, and the preserved cohost format.

Using EO3

Before you begin.

What exactly is a workskin?

A workskin is a stylesheet attached to an AO3 work. It styles your chapter content—messages, letters, headings, and more. The chapter’s HTML supplies the structure; the skin supplies the appearance.

Do I need to know HTML or CSS?

You can write in these examples without editing HTML. Each uses plain text and a small set of markers shown in its guide. The Svelte component handles the structure. Changing the appearance uses CSS; changing a component’s layout uses Svelte.

Can readers turn the formatting off?

AO3 readers can hide the creator’s style. These templates keep names, dates, meaningful labels, and line breaks in HTML, rather than relying on color or generated decoration. Check your own finished fic with the style hidden, too.

Where does my work go?

EO3 saves your work in this browser as you type, and the toolbar says when it’s saved. Use “download” to keep a portable copy of one work, or “Backups” in the sidebar to download everything at once and import it again later. Clearing browser data can remove local saves, so keep a backup. Modules that load external URLs make their own network requests.

Can I go back to an earlier version?

Yes, if you turn it on. Choose “history” in the toolbar and tick “Keep versions of this work.” EO3 then keeps a version for each minute you spend editing and lists what changed in each one, such as words or chapters added. Restore any version, or download it; restoring keeps your current draft as a version too.

What if the preview shows an error?

EO3 names the part that stopped the preview and offers “Open this module” to take you to it. Your writing stays as it is, and the preview comes back once that part is fixed. If your browser blocks copying, EO3 shows the text so you can copy it by hand.

Can I mix examples in one fic?

Yes. Import their groups and connect them to a chapter output. Most formats use distinct class names; text messages and group chats share ChatLog with different layouts. Set a unique Id for each footnote passage, and test the combined work in the editor and on AO3.

What is in the download?

All 14 examples as editable EO3 documents and importable groups, plain-text writing samples, matching chapter HTML fragments, workskin CSS, a usage guide, and the MIT license. The gallery previews are rendered from those same Svelte components. There are no external images or fonts to host.
